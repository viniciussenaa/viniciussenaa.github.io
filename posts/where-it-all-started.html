Initial Evidence

Every investigation has a starting point. Mine didn't begin with malware, digital forensics, or threat intelligence.

It started with an old beige computer, a CRT monitor, an Ubuntu 5.04 CD, and a printed magazine that explained the installation process step by step.

My dad brought that computer home and told me two things: Linux was free, and it didn't get viruses.

I followed the guide, installed Ubuntu, and watched a completely different operating system come to life. I didn't understand kernels, permissions, or open source philosophy back then—but something clicked.

Curiosity came before knowledge.

The Years That Followed

That first experience eventually led me into infrastructure and observability, where I spent more than five years monitoring production environments, responding to incidents, and learning how to read the story hidden inside logs, metrics, and alerts.

Zabbix, Grafana, Datadog, and Prometheus taught me something that I didn't fully appreciate at the time: telemetry is evidence.

Every failed request, every unusual spike, every unexpected process leaves a trace.

The Pivot

Over time, I realized that what interested me most wasn't simply keeping systems available—it was understanding why something happened.

What triggered the alert?

Which artifacts remain after an attack?

How do defenders connect isolated indicators into meaningful intelligence?

Those questions naturally pulled me toward Cyber Threat Intelligence, Digital Forensics, Incident Response, Threat Hunting, and Malware Analysis.

Why Threat Traces Exists

This blog is where I'll document that journey—not as finished expertise, but as an ongoing investigation.

You'll find technical write-ups, DFIR case studies, threat intelligence research, malware analysis, Sigma rules, YARA experiments, and lessons learned from labs and real-world concepts.

Follow the evidence until the attack makes sense.
